Target decisions · what they rest on

1 · Which row is the positive?

The first suspended day: that is where the prior activity is.

attempts declines · sum of the 12 positives, by row relative to the first suspended day

Declines rise row by row and peak on row 0, which summarizes through the previous day. After the suspension, the merchant barely operates.

2 · How are the negatives chosen?

One active day per merchant, with the same date mix as the positives.

positives negatives · % of rows by snapshot month

If the negatives came from any date, the model could learn the calendar instead of the behavior. With the weighted draw, the two curves match.

Why B and not C

C adds ticket and attempts and does not capture more: 9.9 against 10.6. The simpler model stays.

How it is trained

Strong L2 regularization (C = 0.1), balanced class weights, median imputation, and standardized variables. The score is not calibrated: that is why the cuts are percentiles.

Score · what the use of the score rests on

Of every 100 transactions, how many fall in each band and how much of the fraud they bring

% of transactions % of fraud · bands of the merchant's risk percentile

Where the orange bar beats the blue one, the band brings more fraud than its weight: 95–99% (29.5 against 11.7) and 99–100% (6.3 against 2.4).

931 of 2,600frauds in the riskiest 5% · 2,014 merchants

What it says and what it does not

It ranks merchants, not transactions: the riskiest 5% brings 36% of the fraud, but inside a merchant every transaction shares the score. It is trained on suspension, not on fraud. The 0–50% band has a high fraud rate (0.08%): small merchants the score does not see.

Why percentile cuts

The probability is not calibrated: with 0.036% prevalence and heavy classes, 0.5 means nothing. The risk percentile is stable and can be explained to the business.

Pre-authorization rules · what the cuts rest on

Trade-off between the fraud that is avoided and the margin that is lost.

Equilibrium by volume and score rank · it pays if the bar clears the margin (1%)

low volume, 1–9 medium volume, 10–99 high volume, 100 or more · the axis is the score rank

The rule

Low volume with risk ≥ 90%: net benefit $0.24 M, the largest at that level.

Medium volume with risk ≥ 99%: equilibrium 2.3%, benefit $0.14 M.

High volume: never decline. At 90%, 24.9% of good TPV is lost.

The balance is positive

$0.91 M of fraud − $0.53 M of margin = $0.38 M.

CNP stays at $1.5 M through $500 and then falls. At $20,000 fraud stays at 32% and TPV at 12%, about 100 a day. At $5,000 TPV rises to 30%. At $50,000 the two sit near 4%.

What hits no rule is paid immediately. One payment can fall under more than one rule, so the percentages do not add. Together they delay 30.6% of TPV and cover 68.6% of fraud, in 2.13 days. About 28% still passes.

Low · the main one

Approved, with activity and risk below 0.95. Low volume from 0.90 is declined and does not enter. 28,360 merchants.

Medium

High volume with risk from 0.95 to 0.99: a person reviews it. No activity below 0.99: waits 7 days. The rest of that band enters too. 9,582 merchants.

High

Low volume from 0.90, or medium volume from 0.99: declined. Anyone who reaches 0.99 at any volume also enters. 1,411 merchants.
